A slice `[]T` is a growable view of an array. Slices share their buffer on assignment, like in Go,
and the generic helpers in `slices` cover sorting, searching and transforming. The rules are in
[Collections](/docs/learn/collections).

## Creating and appending

`append` returns the new slice, which you assign back. `xs...` appends every element of another
slice.

## Sub-slices and copies

`xs[a..b]` is half-open and shares memory with `xs`. Use `slices.Clone` when you need a separate copy.

<Example id="examples/slices/subslices" />

<Callout type="go">
Zell writes ranges as `xs[1..4]`, `xs[..2]`, `xs[4..]` instead of `xs[1:4]`. There are no built-in
`make`, `copy` or `cap`: use a literal or `slices.Of(v, n)`, and `slices.Clone`.
</Callout>

## Sorting

`slices.Sort` sorts numbers and strings. `SortBy` sorts by a key and `SortFunc` by a `less` function;
both are stable.
